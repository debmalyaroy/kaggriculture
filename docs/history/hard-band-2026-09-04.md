# The HARD band — measuring the fight that decides our rating (2026-09-04)

## Why this exists

On 2026-09-04 the market-layer audit closed the last two levers we had
(`docs/market-layer-audit-2026-09-04.md`): every single-flag market arm was
null or negative at p = 0.0000, and all four fresh top-30 bases tied or
regressed. Both nulls had the same cause, and it is not a property of the
levers.

**Every band we own is saturated where the rating is decided.**

| | held-out panel | ladder, 2300-2500 |
|---|---|---|
| `v44.0_bandit` | **144-0** | **19-36 (0.345)** |

v44.0's only non-wins in 312 panel cells are 15 against `v43.0_bandit`, our
own frozen clone, 12 of them exact $0 draws. It is 297-0 against the other
twelve opponents. Meanwhile 64% of its ladder games are against 2300-2500,
and it loses two thirds of them. Every offline number produced this week
described a fight we already win.

The correction that exposed this is worth recording separately: the
"v44.0 banks 15k/game less than v43.1" regression **does not exist**. Own
bank is a shared-world quantity and matchmaking pairs by rating, so the two
submissions were never in the same competition — **v43.1 has never played an
opponent rated above 2262 in 149 games.** Banded by opponent rating, v44.0
wins more in every window where both have data. Use
`.local/mlayer/live_field.py` before ever comparing two submissions' banks
again.

## What the band is

Not a rating proxy and not a mined roster: **the games we actually played.**
`live_status.episodes()` reads the quota-free EpisodeService, which returns
the episode id, opponent teamId and opponent **pre-game rating** of every
game. 58 episodes against opponents rated >= 2300, **51 distinct teams**,
real ladder worlds, a real 21-37.

    python .local/hardband/harvest.py --download      # index + replays
    python .local/hardband/build_band.py --build      # -> band "hard"
    python .local/hardband/counterfactual.py --calibrate
    python .local/hardband/counterfactual.py agents/v43.1_bandit.py

## What makes it different from every other band

Replays carry `info.seed`, so each cell is played **in its own recorded
world**, not a fresh one. Two consequences:

**1. It self-calibrates.** The agent that played is on disk and both sides
are deterministic, so replaying it must reproduce the recorded banks to the
dollar. It does, in 3 of the first 4 cells checked — world reconstruction,
tape extraction, seat assignment and agent bytes all verified at once. No
other band we own can check itself against anything.

**2. The question is the real one.** Not "does this candidate beat a
2300-rated proxy in a random world" but **"would it have won the games we
lost"**. This is also the only way to measure `v43.1_bandit` in this band at
all: it has never been matched into it, and on its live trend
(0.875 -> 0.860 -> 0.705 as opponent rating rises) it converges near 2300
before it ever gets there.

## The admissibility rule — CERTIFIED cells only

A tape is an open-loop schedule plus a repair layer, and the repair layer is
lossy by construction: it drops hand ops beyond the replaying farm's own
unit count (when its HIREs land differently) and skips non-SELL orders while
broke. In some worlds the tape is therefore **not** a faithful stand-in for
the team that played.

We can tell exactly which worlds. Where the live agent reproduces the
recorded banks to the dollar, the tape is provably faithful there; where it
does not, the cell is measuring the repair layer instead of the opponent.
**Only certified cells are scored** (`--all-cells` overrides, for audit).

This matters in the flattering direction. The one uncertified cell in the
first four (ep 105394728, oppR 2415) turned a recorded 96,474-94,302 **loss**
into a 109,804-74,501 **win** — +13,330 own bank and -19,801 opponent bank,
purely because the tape's hires did not land. An uncertified band would have
reported that as a candidate's success.

## Known bias, stated up front

The opponent cannot react. A candidate that diverges early faces a schedule
compiled for a different agent, so its score is inflated, and certification
bounds this only at the cell level — it proves the tape is faithful to the
**incumbent**, not to a candidate that plays differently. Read a hard-band
result as a **veto and a ranking**, never as a forecast win rate. The
standing rules in `docs/history/gate-calibration-2026-09-03.md` all still apply:
report the held-out half, dedupe seat mirrors, report margin beside cells.

## The defect this band was built to find

Checked on the same 58 replays (`.local/hardband/discriminate.py`) — the
same agent, the same band, 21 wins and 37 losses, which is the win/loss
comparison the standing rule demands:

    OUR volume (SELL clipped at 12/order): mean 1,350  cv 0.028  range 1,156-1,375
    OPP volume (same measure)            : mean 1,519  cv 0.083  range 1,296-2,090
    corr(our volume, opp volume) = +0.175
    opponent out-produced us in 56 of 58 games
    order COUNTS are near-identical: 282 us vs 280 them

    CLIPPING IS REQUIRED. Raw SELL quantities are order INTENT, not fills --
    an oversized SELL is harmless and partially fills (CLAUDE.md), and one
    opponent issues SELL ... 10000. On raw quantities the opponent appears to
    vary 13x and out-produce us by 33%; both are order padding. The real
    figures are 3x and +12.5%. Our own invariance is unaffected either way:
    it is measured on our own orders.

**Our output is a constant.** Every product line is identical in wins and
losses to within 3 units (WHEAT 345 vs 344, STRAWBERRY 267 vs 267, WOOL 138
vs 138); opponents vary about 3x as much. The design intent is "open loop with
adaptive game play" and the adaptive layer has **no measurable authority
over output**. That is why every market-layer arm measured null on
2026-09-04: there was nothing for them to move.

This also killed the fifth hypothesis on the standing test. Wheat looked
decisive in all five fresh loss autopsies (ours 332-380 against a field
median of 500) and separates wins from losses by **+1 unit**. Price regime
does not separate either (0.609 wins, 0.626 losses). What separates is the
opponent's CONVERSION: their bank is 74,863 when we win and 87,634 when we
lose on near-identical order counts (269 vs 285), a mix shift into
MILK/STRAWBERRY/WOOL — the three most glut-sensitive goods (T=122/100/105).
We never contest it because we never change.

NOT yet established, and not to be shipped on: that contesting that mix is
CAUSAL. Stronger opponents bank more whatever they sell. The hard band is
now the place to test it.

## THE INSTRUMENT'S LIMIT, found by the instrument (2026-09-04 16:30)

`v43.1_bandit` scored **29-27 (0.518)** on the certified 56 where `v44.0`
actually went **19-37 (0.339)**. It would have been the best result of the
week. It is an artefact, and the diagnostics catch it cleanly:

| cells | v43.1 | ladder |
|---|---|---|
| plays EXACTLY as the incumbent (28) | **11-17** | **11-17** |
| diverges (28) | 18-10 | 8-20 |

Every extra win is in a divergent cell. On the 11 cells v43.1 flipped from
loss to win, **our own bank fell by a median of $3,167** while the
opponent's fell by **$16,986**; in 7 of the 11 we got poorer and still
"won". The opponent's tape is replaying a schedule compiled for a different
agent and dying. That is not a candidate winning.

**So the raw record is unreadable for any candidate that diverges — which
is every candidate worth testing.** The band is still the best instrument we
have, but only under the rule below.

### CREDITED WINS — the decision number

A win counts only when **our own bank did not fall** to get it and **the
opponent did not collapse** (> -$2,000). Enforced in
`.local/hardband/counterfactual.py`, printed as THE DECISION NUMBER:

    v43.1_bandit  CREDITED  19/56
    v44.1_bandit  CREDITED  19/56
    v44.0_bandit  (ladder)  19/56

**v43.1 does not beat v44.0 in the band that decides our rating -- it EQUALS
it exactly.** (An earlier version of this doc read 17/56 for v43.1. That was
a defect in the metric, not a finding: the credit tolerance was $1, so a win
delivered with $9 less bank was refused. At $500 the three agents tie.)

CONSEQUENCE: at a sane tolerance credited wins SATURATE at 19 for every
variant of this chassis, so it is a VETO channel, not a ranking one. Rank on
the MEDIAN PAIRED MARGIN -- v44.1 ties on credited wins and is still
rejected, because its margin is -1,470 against v44.0's -1,418 and the
reactive holdout scored it 1 better / 18 worse at p = 0.0001. The 148-point live rating gap between them is matchmaking and
path luck, not strength — v43.1 has never met an opponent above 2262, and
when it is finally put in front of that field it produces v44.0's result.

Corollary for the ship decision: **neither live agent is a path to 2800**,
and shipping another layer on this chassis is not one either. The two files
differ by 7 hunks (v44.0 adds the one-key day-6 branch at step 145) and play
IDENTICALLY in 28 of 56 cells; where they differ the effect is mostly noise.
This is the same conclusion the 66-arm splice oracle reached from the other
direction on the same day.

### What this band can and cannot do

* **CAN**: veto a regression; certify that a world is faithful; prove two
  agents are the same policy; measure the incumbent exactly.
* **CANNOT**: rank a divergent candidate on raw wins. Use credited wins, and
  treat a large credited gain with suspicion until it is reproduced against
  a REACTIVE opponent that can answer back.

## LOSS TAXONOMY AND SIX FAILED FIXES (2026-09-04 evening)

All losses classified on 58 real 2300+ games. They are three different
problems, not one:

| class | share | world | our share of pot |
|---|---|---|---|
| narrow (<$3k) | 44% | shared 139k | 50.0% |
| own-collapse (<75k) | 27% | POOR, shared 133k, regime 0.550 | **46.6%** |
| out-banked (>=90k) | 29% | RICH, shared 212k, regime 0.838 | 48.2% |

**Big losses are the opponent playing better, not us breaking.** Our own
final net worth in big losses is only -2,926 below our winning level (-3.6%);
theirs is +15,320 above theirs (+20%).

**Narrow losses are decided by the sell race.** Split by margin (the average
over all games hides this completely):

    close WINS   n=11  %races-won 0.771  net race +$887  lost-2nd   -$643
    close LOSSES n=15  %races-won 0.611  net race   +$27 lost-2nd -$1,813
    blowouts: 0.692 vs 0.664 -- no separation

$860 race gap against a $1,300 median deficit.

### Six fixes built, measured, and rejected

1. `_threat_first` fed by the opponent board -- NEGATIVE, reactive holdout
   1 better / 18 worse, p = 0.0001. Moving it before `_impact_slots` makes it
   byte-identical (that layer re-sorts the queue), so there is no third
   insertion point.
2. `_herd_boost`, the first PRODUCTION-changing layer -- no-op, and when
   forced costs exactly the animal's purchase price for zero return: feeding,
   caring, harvesting and selling are ONE tape-driven schedule.
3. Crop races added to `contested` -- byte-identical. Crop prices sit at
   1.04-1.07 x base, under even the 1.10 contested bar.
4. `_pull_sells` path (b) -- **the opponent-supply trigger promised in the
   docstring since August WAS NEVER IMPLEMENTED**; the code is price-only.
   Implementing it changed nothing: eligible in 2 turns of 592.
5. Wheat admitted to the supply race -- PROPERLY GATED (a first pass called
   it "+$15" off ONE episode, which is exactly the n=1 conclusion the standing
   rule forbids). On all 56 cells it FIRES in 45 and loses: 10 better / 35
   worse, **p = 0.0002**, median -40 / mean -299, raw wins 48 -> 47. This
   vindicates the original exclusion: the codebase drops WHEAT from
   `_PULL_BASE` as "feed-critical", and racing on it measurably costs us.
   The diagnosis below is still right -- the layer is inert BECAUSE our shed
   is wheat -- but the inference "so admit wheat" is wrong.
   WHY THE WHOLE PULL LAYER IS INERT: our shed holds WHEAT (18-62 units) and
   FERTILIZER almost the entire game, and WHEAT is deliberately excluded from
   `_PULL_BASE`. The layer has nothing to accelerate. This explains every
   null `_PULL` measurement, including 2026-09-04's restore at p = 0.0000.
6. `_market_timing` re-tested CONDITIONALLY on world richness (the average
   that retired it in August could have hidden a regime effect) -- negative in
   BOTH: poor worlds 5 better / 22 worse, median -1,522; rich worlds 3/26,
   median -1,559. Stays off.
